# ADR-020: Close Phase 2 and carry the continuity pilot as audited debt
- Date: 2026-09-22
- Status: ACCEPTED
- Authority: product owner
- Supersedes: none
- Amends: none
- Corrects: none
- Rejects: holding P2 open until Gate P1-CONTINUITY is green; abandoning the continuity pilot
- Affects: T-PILOT-01, T-P2-14
- Context: Every P2 exit criterion is met. All 17 of its tasks are DONE, and Gates A, B, C, D and E are green on recorded evidence. The one red gate, P1-CONTINUITY, belongs to P1's exit and not to P2's. It stays red because the continuity pilot in `docs/SPEC.md` is two steps into seven, and T-PILOT-01 has held the single `INTENT.md` slot since 2026-09-21. Phase 3 cannot claim intent while it does. The choice is therefore not whether the pilot matters, but whether it blocks a phase it was never an exit condition for.
- Decision: P2 is COMPLETE as of 2026-09-22. The continuity pilot is parked rather than abandoned: T-PILOT-01 returns to TODO, its two passing criteria stand, its contract stays frozen under the rule that froze it, and no criterion is weakened or dropped. Gate P1-CONTINUITY stays RED and P1 stays EXIT_PENDING. The remaining five criteria are recorded as procedure debt with a named payoff point: the Phase 3 exit audit runs the pilot procedure against the Phase 3 work itself, in this repository and across the real multi-session build, rather than restarting it in a disposable project. A live phase exercises cross-session compliance, checkpoint behaviour, and shared understanding more honestly than a Tea Timer does.
- Consequences: P1 does not close when P2 closes, and the two phases stay recorded separately, as they already were. The debt is visible in three places that a compile reports rather than in prose alone: the red gate, the open task, and this decision. The risk accepted is real and is stated rather than hidden — Phase 3 will be built on a workflow whose cross-session agent compliance is still unverified, so a pilot failure at audit lands against P3 work already written. That cost was weighed against leaving a finished phase open behind a gate that does not govern it. The disposable pilot project at /tmp/pilot is no longer load-bearing; `docs/pilot-2026-09-21.md` keeps what steps 1 and 2 observed.
